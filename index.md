<h2 style="margin: 60px 0px 10px;">Short Bio</h2>

I am a sixth-year PhD candidate in Political Science at the University of Chicago. My research lies in comparative and historical political economy, with a primary focus on China. I study the long-run evolution of China’s political economy, particularly how political commitment evolves with power dynamics and, in turn, shapes state capacity and economic development from the imperial period to the present. More broadly, I use this framework to explain divergent trajectories of political and economic development across societies. I primarily use game-theoretic models, complemented by historical narratives and empirical evidence. My dissertation committee includes Scott Gehlbach (chair), Wioletta Dziuda, Joel Mokyr, Monika Nalepa, Kenneth Pomeranz, and James Robinson.
